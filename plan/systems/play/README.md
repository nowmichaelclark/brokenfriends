# Play

## In charge of

Toys, minigames, and how friends grow their skills and stats (and may
learn new skills) by playing.

## Not in charge of

- Poking, feeding and the other care gestures: `care-touch`.
- Needs like energy that limit how much play is possible:
  `needs-growth`.
- Using the skills on quests: `expeditions`.

## Seed notes (from Mikey, 2026-10-24)

- "Play" instead of "train" (decision 0017): "Train sounds like a gym;
  yes their stats can increase from playing, but it is still largely
  playing through which the friends develop their skills or perhaps
  even learn new skills."
- Friends should be fun to poke and listen to, with toys, like
  Nintendogs (Q-023).
- References: Nintendogs; Chao Garden and Monster Rancher for stats
  that grow.

- The play corner (recording 1, 2026-10-24): a small corner of the
  tent where a friend sits, sleeps, and plays with a ball or a small
  skateboard; later more toys (Mikey: "we could have say 100 toys").
- Toys need more than one interaction (recording 2): a ball hits the
  whole body, a foot sometimes kicks it back, a trained friend still
  drops it; friends could throw and kick it to each other. A
  trampoline lets a friend trip at first and bounce high later.
- Skill numbers stay mostly hidden; casual players just play (0033).
- Friends speak in noises that sound like talk but aren't, R2-D2
  style, not robotic voices (recording 1).

## Mini games (0043, 0044; Mikey, 2026-10-25)

- Real mini games, not only toys: a goal, something to get better at,
  a way to fail; a minute or two, one thumb, portrait, no words.
- Mikey liked all of Claude's first list: fishing at the river (catch
  thread, buttons, a boot, now and then a lost arm), a tower of friends
  (stack them to reach something high), keepy-uppy between friends,
  the trampoline, a jam session to Driftloom's music, a patchwork
  puzzle (scraps fit over a hole become the patch), hide and seek.
- Route mini games, one per kind of route: crossing the river
  (Frogger-like), climbing over rocks, swinging across gaps.
- Two skateboarding games: a grind and obstacle course like OlliOlli,
  and a half pipe for tricks and high scores.
- The framework (0044): mini games ask for
  abilities worked out from the body (size, weight, grip, push, jump,
  balance, float), never for parts; the camera zooms so every friend
  shows at about the same size, and each size has games it's best at;
  the player times, the friend's skill widens the window; each game
  says what it gives (skill, prizes, both) and what it risks (a loose
  seam, a lost part, mud, soaking), shown back at camp.
- Built on the soft ragdolls of `prototypes/04-physics-test.md`.
- A body that truly can't play (rare): a shrug, and the game closes
  (0044).
- A head that comes off mid-game usually ends the game; "there might
  be some exceptions to that or a short time period of leniency"
  (Mikey, Q-034).
- Limbs could be creative things, "a limb as a broom or a limb as a
  clawshot" (Mikey, 2026-10-25): unshaped, "something we can play
  with".
- The play room (Mikey, Q-036): a decently sized room where friends
  wander, "the more the merrier", with the friend count found by a
  stress test on the phone; the camera zooms in and out.
- Later versions won't open every mini game at the start; the dev
  release does (proposed 0045; how that fits 0010: Q-040).
