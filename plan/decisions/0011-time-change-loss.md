# 0011: Time passes; change happens; loss is possible

## Decision

Time passes while the player is away. A creature can change and can
die, at camp or in the open world. Death is final. Absence alone never
kills a healthy creature.

## Context

The second Brain session had to settle Q-011: how time away can change
a creature without it feeling like loss, when nothing is gated. At
least one pillar depends on the answer. The session also settled Q-007
(can a creature die?) on the way.

## Why

- Mikey: "loss can happen for sure. But change WILL happen."
- Accepting death, loss and change is a central theme of the game.
- The creatures are fuzzy, almost like soft robots, which lightens the
  tone; it is still heavy for very young players, and that is accepted.
- A careful player who tends their creatures and picks quests wisely
  could keep a collection alive a long time, but not by luck alone.
- Rot needs a source, so every death has a visible history and a player
  can see why (pillar 3).

## The rules as settled

- **Rot needs a source:** a wound, mud, bugs, an unclean camp. Nothing
  rots from nothing.
- **Dirt speeds rot; it never starts it.** A broken broom means a
  dirtier camp and faster rot on a creature already wounded. It never
  means death from a clean, healthy creature.
- **At camp:** a healthy creature is never harmed by time alone. A
  creature left with an untended wound worsens and can die.
- **On a quest:** a creature can die, or come back too broken to
  return. Another creature can be sent to retrieve it.
- **No revive.** A retrieved body brings back parts, not the friend.
- **Keepsakes:** a fully dead creature leaves a few parts behind: a
  button eye, a leg, a cape. Higher-quality parts are more likely to
  survive. That is the only thing death leaves behind.
- **Parts can be reattached and donated,** including across creatures
  (see decision on the pillars and pillar 2).
- **The camp is cleaned with one tap,** using tools and resources that
  can break and can be found in the open world.

## Alternatives rejected

- **No offline change at all:** contradicts the pitch.
- **A healthy creature can start rotting while away ("rare is fine"):**
  a weekly player carries a compounding risk, and it reads as the game
  holding them, which decision 0010 refuses.
- **Retirement or wandering off instead of death:** softer, but it
  dodges the theme Mikey wants.
- **Revive, at a cost:** turns loss into an errand.

## Consequences

- `00-vision/pitch.md` says change and loss "can be affected by the
  player", not "the promise is change, not loss".
- Pillars 2, 3 and 4 rest on this.
- Needs systems later: condition, camp upkeep, tools, quests and
  retrieval, keepsakes, donation.
- Raises Q-016 to Q-020.
