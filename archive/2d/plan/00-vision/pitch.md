# Pitch

Fits on one phone screen.

## One sentence

Broken Friends is a mobile pet game about stitched creatures that fall
apart. *(Decision 0008's words; 0022 now calls them friends.)*

## Who it's for, and how they play

Kids should love it, and adults and experienced players should too.
Mikey: "a lot of times these pet games are aimed exclusively at kids,
and we want to make a pet game where it's like, okay, kids can enjoy
this, but it's not like a dumb game." Little reading is needed (0032),
so children who can't read yet, and players of any language, can play.

Two play patterns, both supported:

- **Daily or weekly check-ins:** a short visit. Feed, care, repair,
  poke, maybe one minigame. Still a nice experience.
- **Longer sessions:** more friends, more play, and quests: the main
  way the game moves forward (0034). A player may also just stay at
  camp and play.

It's free. Nothing is gated. No microtransactions, no login streaks,
no energy timers, no nagging notifications (0024). The game is not
trying to hold the player to something. Players come back more often because they
grow attached to a particular friend or a particular goal.

Time away can change a friend, and a player can always get a new
friend. Change and loss both happen here, and both can be affected
by the player (decision 0011).

## The first five minutes

The player is given a friend with a rotting leg. The player rips the
leg off themselves (direct and hands-on, like Trauma Center on the DS
V-012), then sews on a replacement. They feed it, care
for it and poke it, and watch it react.

Repair is shown before any menu.

## What makes it different

Most pet games promise you can restore your pet. In Broken Friends,
friends fall apart, and taking care of them means ripping off what's
rotting, sewing on replacements, and changing and adding parts. They're
never quite the same afterward. They're still your friends.

There is no going back to the friend as you found it. A friend may
end up a Theseus friend: same friend, different parts. Parts can even
move between friends: a healthy leg given to a friend who has none.

## The player's role

The player is a character of sorts, closer to a benevolent doctor than
to a god. They befriend and care for the friends, then send them on
quests. The goal behind the quests: set up camps in every biome of a
map-only world, and befriend the friends who live there (0035).

The doctor is silent; the friends do the talking. The doctor is
lonely and has no other friends. These lively, toy-like friends are
almost imaginary friends to them.

They are friends we play with and care for, not things we make. A
tool for making a friend from scratch exists for testing, and may
become a late-game unlock (0036).

## The feeling to leave with

"It's nice taking care of these odd friends." Mikey, after playing the
sketch: fun, wholesome, silly, a little strange, and "oh, I'd like to
come back and see how my creatures are doing again", without the game
being built to be addictive.

Tone: wholesome and gross, in the spirit of Tim Burton's stop-motion
films [unverified, V-013]: stitched, patched, a little rotten, still
lovable.

The arc, such as it is: the doctor learns to accept death, and to be
happy with the friends they have now. There is no plot around it to
resolve. One ending scene plays when the last camp is unlocked, and
play goes on after (0035).

## Still open in this pitch

- Quests, camps and biomes are decided in outline (0034, 0035), not
  designed.
- The part system is decided in outline (0025, 0026, 0027), not
  designed.
