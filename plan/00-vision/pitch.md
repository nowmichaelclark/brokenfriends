# Pitch

Fits on one phone screen.

## One sentence

Broken Friends is a mobile pet game about stitched creatures that fall
apart.

## Who it's for, and how they play

Cozy players who want a low-pressure daily ritual (a few minutes a day,
no pressure, nothing gated), with dark-cute, odd-things fans as the
flavor. *(The model's wording, not Mikey's: see Q-015.)*

Two play patterns, both supported:

- **Daily or weekly check-ins:** a short visit. Feed, care, repair,
  poke, maybe one minigame. Still a nice experience.
- **Longer sessions:** more creatures, more minigames, quests, and the
  overarching goals behind the quest system.

Nothing is gated. No microtransactions. The game is not trying to hold
the player to something. Players come back more often because they
grow attached to a particular creature or a particular goal.

Time away can change a creature, and a player can always get a new
creature. Change and loss both happen here, and both can be affected
by the player (decision 0011).

## The first five minutes

The player is given a creature with a rotting arm. The player rips the
arm off themselves (direct and hands-on, like Trauma Center on the DS
[unverified, V-012]), then sews on a replacement. They feed it, care
for it and poke it, and watch it react.

Repair is shown before any menu.

## What makes it different

Most pet games promise you can restore your pet. In Broken Friends,
creatures fall apart, and taking care of them means ripping off what's
rotting, sewing on replacements, and changing and adding parts. They're
never quite the same afterward. They're still your friends.

There is no going back to the creature as you found it. A creature may
end up a Theseus creature: same friend, different parts. Parts can even
move between friends: a healthy leg given to a creature who has none.

## The player's role

The player is a character of sorts, closer to a benevolent doctor than
to a god. They care for and befriend creatures, then send them on
quests. Later we find out the player has goals they can achieve through
the creatures.

The doctor is silent; the creatures do the talking. The doctor is
lonely and has no other friends. These lively, toy-like creatures are
almost imaginary friends to them.

Creatures are friends we play with and care for, not things we make.

A "make your own creature" feature may come later, so nothing here
rules it out.

## The feeling to leave with

"It's nice taking care of these odd friends."

Tone: wholesome and gross, in the spirit of Tim Burton's stop-motion
films [unverified, V-013]: stitched, patched, a little rotten, still
lovable.

The arc, such as it is: the doctor learns to accept death, and to be
happy with the friends they have now. There is no plot around it to
resolve.

## Still open in this pitch

- Quests and the overarching goals are promised, not designed.
- "Change and add parts" is deliberately abstract; the part system is
  unwritten, including donation (Q-016) and keepsakes (Q-017).
- The open world, multiple camps and tool scavenging are parked
  (Q-018).
