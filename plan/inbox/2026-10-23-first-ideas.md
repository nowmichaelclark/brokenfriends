# First ideas (2026-10-23)

Raw material from Mikey's first planning chat, summarized by Claude.
**Not decisions.** Brain sessions sort these into docs and questions.

## The game

- Like a Tamagotchi, but far more physical and interactive.
- Pull a creature's limbs apart, and sew them back together.
- Overfeed it and watch it get really big; underfeed it and watch it
  get skinny.
- Creatures that thrive in water, and creatures that thrive on land.
- Minigames with your creatures that train them.
- Later, send them on quests to pick up and deliver packages, like the
  carrier robots in Death Stranding.
- Mechanics and systems not fully thought out yet.

## The creatures

- Stuffed, stitched creatures: a teddy-bear-like creature, the
  stitched dolls from the film *9*, the rag-doll heroine of *The
  Nightmare Before Christmas* (who sews her own limbs back on).
- They can get wet, grow mold, and have fluff inside that can fall out.

## Platforms and tech

- Completely 2D.
- Mobile first; also playable in a browser; maybe native Windows,
  Linux and Mac later.
- Godot suggested as the engine (not yet confirmed by Mikey).
- An earlier plan suggested "gym, zoo, museum" test scenes: a gym for
  tuning movement, a zoo for showing assets and interactions, a museum
  for demonstrating systems.

## Music

- Bring Driftloom's Rust core into the game later.
- Each creature gets its own randomly generated theme song.
- Other generated music changes with the time of day and with what's
  happening in the game.

## Ideas from the chat worth considering (Claude's suggestions, unconfirmed)

- Wetness makes a creature heavier and slower at carrying; an
  overstuffed creature is sturdier but slower.
- Ripped seams and patches read as charming rather than gory.
- A creature's theme could be a stored Driftloom song code, since a
  code always renders the same song.
- Reference games: Chao Garden, Monster Rancher, Digimon World,
  Pikmin, Rain World, LittleBigPlanet, Kirby's Epic Yarn and others
  (see `00-vision/references.md`).
