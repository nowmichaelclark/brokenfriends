## To become decisions (proposed)

1. **Any part can come off.** Healthy parts too, but they hold: it takes
   several hard pulls (about three), more for strong parts.
2. **Donation leaves the donor worse off**, just by missing the part: a
   one-legged friend hops or crawls. Nothing extra on top. (Q-016)
3. **Things are scarce.** Parts, toys and tools are found on quests, not
   lying around; even stuffing and oil run out. So the player chooses
   whom to care for most, and a neglected friend may die.
4. **Parts can die on their own**: rotted through, soaked, torn up, a
   cracked button. Past a point, a part can't be repaired.
5. **The personality lives in the head** (the chip brain). A loved
   friend with a ruined body can have its head moved onto another body.
6. **How a friend dies**: not surviving a quest, or falling apart at
   camp (rot, no stuffing, leaking fluid, bugs, worms, mold, water,
   rust reach the brain). Not easy, but a semi-regular part of play.
7. **A dead friend's parts can be reused.** Died at camp: nearly all
   parts. Died on a quest: one or two (something scavenged the rest).
   The friend itself never comes back (0011). (Q-017)
8. **Gross is playful; there is no line to find.** Playing with gross
   things is wholesome, like a kid playing with food. The tone fence
   still stands: no blood or gore. (Q-004)
9. **Front and back only, never the side.** In the play area friends
   walk toward and away from the screen and flip to their back. The
   back is used too: tail and brain surgery, capes.
10. **Almost no text.** The game should play without reading (settings
    menus aside), so non-readers and non-English speakers can play.
    The sketch's hint text is temporary.
11. **Time away (Q-021)**: how tidy the camp was left decides how likely
    loss is; hunger never kills but speeds rot and makes quests more
    likely to fail; mold left on a rotting part spreads to others.
12. **Finding out what happened (Q-022)**: by looking, never a report.
    Mud tracked across the floor, bugs, mold that spread, a friend
    back from a quest still soaked and sitting weakly.
13. **Skills grow by repetition, quietly.** Playing builds skills on
    the side; numbers are mostly hidden, only implied; nobody is pushed
    to pick a toy for its stat. (Builds on 0017.)
14. **Quests are a main promise (Q-012)** and arrive in the first ten
    minutes or so. A player may still just stay home and play.
15. **Quest risk is shown on a map (Q-020)**, Death Stranding style: the
    route, a success chance, danger signs (rivers, spikes). The player
    can draw a safer long route or a risky short one, and add side
    trips (a treasure chest) that bring their own risk.
16. **New friends arrive** looking for help or a home, or are brought
    home by a friend from a quest. Now and then (1 in 5 to 10) a
    newcomer brings a toy or supplies.
17. **The map-only open world is in, early (Q-018).** Many camps: at
    least one per biome, ideally easy, hard and extreme ones. New camps
    are found by questing further out and cost materials to build.
    Biomes reward new kinds of creature and biome-only toys.
18. **Friends mostly stay at their camp.** Moving them is allowed but
    limited (perhaps one at a time at first), so every camp keeps some
    friends to look after it. Mikey may drop this.
19. **Who it's for (Q-015)**: kids should love it, and adults and
    experienced players should too; not a dumbed-down kids' game.
20. **Story (Q-019)**: no campaign. The loose arc: be a good doctor, set
    up camps in every biome, befriend the creatures. Maybe one ending
    scene.
21. **Making a creature from scratch (Q-013)**: built first as a
    developer tool; maybe later a late-game unlock (machines that make
    skin, limbs, and last of all a brain).
22. **Art is made by maths (Q-006)**, weighted like Driftloom: common
    and rare features, developer sliders. Great variety (many kinds of
    head, and creatures that aren't animals: monsters, ghosts, demons,
    aliens) that still looks like one game. Bodies move by their build:
    thin ones wobble, plump ones bounce, thin ears droop and swing;
    hair with physics, like Sally's.
23. **Every friend sounds different**, across thousands of friends.
24. **Music changes at moments that matter**, as in a console game, not
    on a timer: a room change, a friend made well, a mood, the time of
    day, surgery, a death. Changes double as quiet signs of success;
    calm enough to sit and listen.
25. **Non-goals kept**: no blood or gore, no pain the player can't
    treat, no revive, no story campaign.
26. **Single player first, fully playable offline.** Sharing creatures
    by a code is wanted; online play is left room for, not planned.
27. **Combat, leaning no**: no swords or guns. Friends avoid and deter
    danger (agility, gadgets like a claw shot). Wild animals can hurt
    them (a wolf tears off a limb).
28. **Chores yes**: a friend can be given a standing job, like keeping
    supplies spread between camps, and can get hurt doing it.
29. **First playable**: the sketch is close. Add the core loop at one
    camp: quests, friends arriving, friends dying.
30. **Surgery happens lying down, in its own place.** The standing view
    is for washing and accessories; surgery gets its own table and
    tools.

## Doc text (no decision needed)

  gross but with their own charm).

## Feedback on the sketch (for the build, 0021)

- Healthy limbs should tear after repeated hard pulls (item 1).
- Show fluid leaking where a limb was torn off.
- Surgery is a little too easy (fine for the first) and the camera
  doesn't get close enough; it should be up close with the stitches.
- The ball got boring in about 20 seconds: learning to catch was too
  quick, a trained friend should still fumble, and the ball should hit
  the whole body (kick it back when it hits a foot).
- The music changing every loop is wrong (item 24).
- Arms and legs look like a paper doll, not a fluffy creature.
- Surgery should be lying down, in its own place (item 30).
